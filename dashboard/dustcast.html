Read this before quoting a number

  • 630 MW is 500 MW residential rooftop plus ~130 MW on MV/HV networks, both STEG's (end-June 2026). The split into three segments, into governorates and into units is ours — allocated by population, which probably over-weights Tunis and under-weights the Sahel.
  • The fleet is simulated: five sampled installations per segment per governorate, with plausible tilt, azimuth and capacity. No installation-level Tunisian data was available to this project. The upscaling method is the one AEMO and Sheffield Solar use on real registries.
  • The 266 units are prototypes, built from delegation counts. They are not verified STEG operational units, which are more likely defined by substations and MV feeders than by administrative boundaries.
  • The model is L0 physics — pvlib on forecast weather, no learned correction. Every model that beats it learns from a site's own measured output, and no rooftop here has any. Its measured error, at DKASC in Australia, is 5.6% nMAE intra-day and 6.7% at J+1. Tunisian accuracy is unvalidated.
  • The band is an illustrative range, not a validated interval. The per-site band is conformal-calibrated at DKASC, where measured coverage is 94.1%, 92.1% and 86.4% on three arrays against 90% nominal. Aggregating those across regions assumes equal error scales and one common correlation, treats half-widths as standard deviations, and estimates the correlation from forecast clear-sky index as a proxy for forecast-error correlation. National coverage is not established.
  • Reserve, ramp and range tiles are scenario estimates from the simulated fleet. "Reserve to hold" is the widest half-range anywhere in the displayed window, which falls in a low-sun hour, not at peak output.
