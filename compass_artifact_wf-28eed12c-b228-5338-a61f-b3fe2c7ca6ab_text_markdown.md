# Forecasting Rooftop Solar in Tunisia: State-of-the-Art Review and Ranked Hackathon Project Concepts

## TL;DR
- The most creative-yet-feasible angle for this track is **NOT a better point forecaster but a decision-support platform built around three under-exploited pillars: (1) distribution-free uncertainty via conformal prediction, (2) region-specific Saharan-dust/soiling modeling using free Copernicus CAMS aerosol forecasts, and (3) spatial "upscaling" from a few monitored sites to a national estimate** — all three are achievable with free data (Open-Meteo, NASA POWER, PVGIS, CAMS, DKASC desert data) and open-source tools (pvlib, MAPIE, NeuralForecast, Open Climate Fix's quartz-solar-forecast).
- Recent 2024–2026 literature is clear on two points that should shape strategy: **gradient boosting (XGBoost/LightGBM) plus a conformal wrapper is a genuinely competitive baseline** that transformers and time-series foundation models (Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai) frequently fail to beat for site-level solar — so differentiation should come from the *system* around the forecast, not from chasing the biggest model; and **time-series foundation models' real value is zero/few-shot "cold-start" forecasting for sites with no history**, which maps perfectly onto Tunisia's data-scarce reality.
- Tunisia specifics make the dust/data-scarcity angle authentic: national PV reached **895 MW in 2025 (up from 775 MW in 2024, IRENA Renewable Capacity Statistics 2026)**, residential rooftop PV is **~500 MW plus 130 MW on medium/high-voltage networks by end-June 2026 (STEG)**, **STEG/ANME publish no open generation or per-site installation data** (unlike Australia's CER postcode data), and surplus sales to STEG are capped at 30% of annual production (Decree 2016-1123) — so a platform that works with sparse monitoring and free satellite/reanalysis data is exactly what the national operator lacks.

## Key Findings

### On models
- For **site-level and short-horizon PV forecasting, tree-based gradient boosting remains a top or near-top performer** while being 1–2 orders of magnitude cheaper to train than transformers. A Tunisia-focused 2025 study (Rjim Matouk site) found a Prophet+mutual-information pipeline beat iTransformer, PatchTST, TimesFM, Chronos and SOFTS on MAPE with ~96% lower training time, and flagged transformers'/foundation models' 6–13 h training and 156–507 ms inference as barriers to operational use.
- **PatchTST is the most consistently strong transformer for solar** because its patching aligns with the 24-h diurnal cycle; iTransformer wins mainly in high-dimensional multi-site/cross-variate settings. Both only sometimes beat boosting, and only with enough data.
- **Time-series foundation models (Chronos-2, TimesFM 2.5, Moirai 2.0, TiRex, TabPFN-TS, Lag-Llama, Tiny Time Mixers) work zero-shot** but are beaten by simple methods on high-frequency data; their standout use case is **cold-start / no-history sites**, especially when seeded with a physics-based synthetic history (pvlib/PVGIS).
- **Spatio-temporal Graph Neural Networks** exploit the fact that nearby PV sites are "virtual weather stations" sharing cloud fields; they beat isolated per-site models especially for multi-hour-ahead regional forecasts (GCLSTM/GCTrafo on 300+ Swiss sites; DEST-GNN, Fourier-GNN, terrain-aware GCN-LSTM in 2024–2026).
- **Very-short-term nowcasting (1–30 min)** is dominated by sky-image and satellite methods: cloud-motion vectors are being replaced by deep learning (HelioNet U-Net on Meteosat MSG; SolarFormer; DGMR-style generative nowcasting). Open datasets exist (SKIPP'D, and a 72-dataset survey). Meteosat MSG/SEVIRI covers North Africa at 1×1 km HRV / 15-min.
- **Physics-informed / hybrid ML is the highest-ROI "serious" technique for a hackathon**: use a pvlib clear-sky model (Ineichen–Perez) as a physical baseline and train ML on the *residual*. Consistently improves accuracy, interpretability and transfer across climates.

### On uncertainty
- **Conformal prediction is the single most practical, high-payoff uncertainty method** for this track: it is distribution-free, model-agnostic, wraps any point model, and gives finite-sample coverage guarantees. Multiple 2024–2026 PV papers use it (Renkema et al. on day-ahead PV + market bidding; adaptive conformal inference with a PV-specific daily miscoverage reset; PatchTST+ACI). Library: MAPIE.
- **NGBoost** gives a full predictive distribution from a single boosting model (CRPS 21–43% better than quantile regression in a 2025 ultra-short-term PV study), but its intervals can be mis-calibrated — best combined with a conformal calibration step.
- Grid operators care about **CRPS, pinball/quantile loss, interval coverage (PICP), sharpness (PINAW), Winkler score, and ramp-detection skill — not plain RMSE.**

### On the spatial/upscaling problem (Tunisia's actual situation)
- **PV "upscaling"** — estimating regional/national output from a small representative sample of monitored systems — is exactly how real operators handle invisible distributed PV: AEMO's ASEFS2 (Himawari + PVOutput + CER capacity), UK Sheffield Solar/PVLive + Open Climate Fix's PVNet, Elia/TenneT, and Pierro et al.'s clustering+NN method for Italy (3% RMSE estimation, 5–7% intra-day).
- **Behind-the-meter PV disaggregation** (separating solar from net load) is the complementary technique; 2024–2026 work uses unsupervised/semi-supervised separation, needing <10% observable customers.
- **Rooftop PV detection from imagery** (DeepSolar, DeepSolar++, Deep Active Learning) can build the missing installation registry, but models transfer poorly across regions — and there is essentially one prior case study in Tunisia, making it a genuine research gap.

### On the region-specific angle (dust, soiling, heat)
- **Saharan dust is a first-order, genuinely Tunisian driver** rarely modeled in generic solar projects. Micheli et al. (Sustainable Energy Technologies and Assessments 62, 2024) documented that the March 2022 storm **halved the Spanish national PV fleet's capacity factor for more than two weeks, with an 80% peak-day drop** (PV met only 7% of demand instead of the expected 11%). In West Africa, Copernicus ACP 25:997 (2025) found a 2021 dry-season plume caused an **average 18% reduction in surface solar irradiance**, and that accounting for dust cut GHI estimation error by ~75%. Soiling accumulates fastest in arid climates (IEA-PVPS Task 13).
- **Copernicus CAMS provides free 5-day forecasts of dust aerosol optical depth (AOD)** at 0.4°, hourly to 120 h, via the Atmosphere Data Store — usable to anticipate dust-driven PV drops and to schedule panel cleaning. **DustNet (Nowak et al., *Geoscientific Model Development* 18(11):3509–3532, 13 Jun 2025)** is a 2D CNN on ERA5+MODIS that **trains in under 8 minutes and predicts in 2.1 s on a desktop without a GPU**, and **outperforms the physics-based CAMS forecast at 95% of grid locations** for 24-h Saharan-dust AOD (annual spatial-mean r²=0.91 vs CAMS 0.71).

### On data availability (the make-or-break for feasibility)
- **Free and North-Africa-covering:** Open-Meteo (free forecast + historical + satellite-radiation APIs incl. EUMETSAT MSG/SARAH3 over Africa; global tilted irradiance; CAMS air-quality/dust); NASA POWER (global, hourly since 2001 for solar, free API); PVGIS (Europe/Africa, TMY + hourly, free); CAMS radiation service (Meteosat field of view = Europe/Africa/Middle East, free with registration); ERA5/ERA5-Land.
- **Desert/semi-arid proxy PV datasets that transfer to Tunisia:** DKASC Alice Springs (15+ years, 40+ arrays, 5-min/10-s, free — the single best desert analogue), Adrar (Algeria) studies, NREL PVDAQ. Global multi-climate: Ausgrid, UK-PV/Sheffield Solar, PVOutput.org.
- **Tunisia-specific:** national PV 895 MW (2025, IRENA); residential rooftop PV ~500 MW + 130 MW on MV/HV networks (STEG, end-June 2026); **no open STEG/ANME generation or installation dataset**; AERONET aerosol stations at Tunis-Carthage, Ben Salem, Medenine; surplus-to-STEG capped at 30% of annual production.
- **Hackathon-accelerating libraries:** pvlib-python, Open Climate Fix's quartz-solar-forecast (pip-installable; a gradient-boosted-tree model using 9 Open-Meteo NWP variables, trained on 25,000 PV sites with 5+ years of history, ~13% MAE excluding night) and PVNet, Nixtla NeuralForecast/StatsForecast, GluonTS, Darts, MAPIE (conformal), sktime, NGBoost.

## Details

### 1. State of the art in PV forecasting (2024–2026)

**Probabilistic / uncertainty-aware forecasting.** The clearest practical winner is conformal prediction (CP). It is distribution-free and model-agnostic: you build any point forecaster, then calibrate prediction intervals on a held-out set with finite-sample coverage guarantees. Renkema, Visser & AlSkaif (Solar Energy Advances, 2024) applied CP to day-ahead PV and found CP with k-NN and Mondrian binning after random-forest regression gave the best-calibrated intervals; a companion paper (Electric Power Systems Research, 2024) showed CP + expected-utility bidding captured up to 93% of potential market profit. Adaptive conformal inference (ACI) handles distribution shift; a 2026 Energies study introduced a PV-specific daily miscoverage reset to stop uncertainty accumulating across night hours, and found linear models + ACI matched an LSTM at far lower cost. A separate 2025 Energies paper paired PatchTST with ACI. Recommended library: MAPIE. NGBoost (Stanford ML Group) gives a full Gaussian predictive distribution from one model and beat quantile regression by 21–43% CRPS in a 2025 ultra-short-term PV study, but practitioners note its intervals need a conformal calibration layer.

**Foundation models.** Chronos-2, TimesFM 2.5, Moirai 2.0, TiRex, TabPFN-TS and Lag-Llama produce zero-shot forecasts. Benchmarks show they are frequently beaten by tuned statistical/boosting models on high-frequency data (only Chronos-2 and TimesFM-2.5 beat AutoTheta on second-frequency data in GIFT-Eval), but become competitive at lower frequencies. Their genuinely novel application to PV is **cold-start**: a June 2026 arXiv paper ("Time series Foundation Models based on Physics-Informed Synthetic Histories") generates a pvlib/PVGIS-based synthetic production history from plant metadata (tilt, azimuth, capacity) and feeds it as context to zero-shot foundation models to forecast brand-new plants with no history — evaluated on 440 sites including DKASC desert data. This is a strong "creative" angle for Tunisia's many unmonitored new rooftops.

**Transformers vs boosting.** PatchTST (patching + channel independence) is the most reliable transformer for solar because its patches align with the diurnal cycle; iTransformer excels in high-dimensional cross-variate settings but is weak univariate. The critical evaluation literature (including the Tunisia Rjim Matouk study) repeatedly finds these do not reliably beat gradient boosting or even Prophet for site-level solar, at much higher compute cost. Message for the team: use boosting as the baseline and only add a transformer if multi-site cross-learning is central.

**Graph Neural Networks.** Simeunović et al. (IEEE TSTE, 2022) introduced GCLSTM/GCTrafo, modeling 300+ PV sites as signals on a graph and beating isolated LSTMs for up to 4–6 h ahead; the key intuition is that PV plants form "a dense network of virtual weather stations" capturing cloud motion. 2024–2026 extensions: DEST-GNN (adaptive adjacency, intra-hour), Fourier-GNN, and terrain-feature GCN-LSTM (2026). GNNs are the natural fit for the "multiple spatial scales" requirement but are the highest-risk build in a hackathon.

**Nowcasting (1–30 min).** Ground sky-imagers (SKIPP'D dataset, SUNSET CNN, SkyGPT generative model) and satellite methods co-exist. HelioNet (Straub et al., Solar RRL 2024) is a U-Net that predicts future Meteosat MSG cloud-index images, replacing cloud-motion vectors, benchmarked to 4 h ahead. SolarFormer and DGMR-style generative models (open-sourced) push probabilistic satellite nowcasting. Meteosat MSG/SEVIRI covers North Africa (HRV 1×1 km, 15 min) — directly relevant to Tunisia — and Open-Meteo's satellite-radiation API already serves EUMETSAT-derived radiation over Africa.

**Physics-informed ML.** The highest-ROI serious method: use pvlib's clear-sky model (Ineichen–Perez) or a full pvlib ModelChain as the physical baseline, then train ML (XGBoost/LSTM) on the residual (the cloud/aerosol-driven deviation). Pombo et al. (DTU) showed physics-informed features consistently dominate; a 2025 South-African study reported 93% empirical coverage of 95% intervals and good transfer across stations with clear-sky-constrained residual learning. This directly satisfies the brief's "leveraging weather data and solar installation information."

### 2. Spatial scaling / upscaling
Real operators solve Tunisia's exact problem — invisible distributed PV — by upscaling from a representative sample: national/regional generation ≈ measured yield of reference systems × total installed capacity. AEMO's ASEFS2 combines Himawari imagery, PVOutput.org + Solar Analytics reference systems, and CER postcode capacity. The UK stack (Sheffield Solar PVLive targets + Open Climate Fix PVNet, a multi-modal NWP+satellite+metadata model) forecasts regional GB PV; OCF's national_pv_gsp_sum sums GSP-region predictions. Pierro et al. (2017, Italy) cluster the PV fleet and use per-cluster NNs on satellite/NWP inputs: 3% RMSE for estimation, 5–7% intra-day. BTM disaggregation (separating solar from net load) is the complement, with 2024 unsupervised methods working at <10% observability. Rooftop detection (DeepSolar/DeepSolar++, Deep Active Learning needing only ~3% of labels) can rebuild the missing registry, but cross-region transfer is poor and Tunisia is nearly unstudied — a real gap.

### 3. Grid impact and decision support
Forecast uncertainty is consumed by operators as reserve sizing (a 3σ rule on net-demand forecast error; each +1% reserve cost CAISO ~$50M/yr historically), ramp risk (the duck-curve neck can require ~13,000 MW in 3 h; a 35% ramp-forecast improvement cut CAISO spinning-reserve cost ~25%/$5M in one study), curtailment risk, and distribution-feeder voltage. **Decision-focused learning (DFL) / smart predict-then-optimize** trains the forecaster with a loss aligned to the downstream decision, not RMSE — Elmachtoub & Grigas' SPO; Donti et al.'s task-based learning; a 2024 ACM e-Energy "decision-focused retraining"; PV-battery scheduling and reserve-market papers. Cost-sensitive/asymmetric losses (pinball, CRPS, Winkler) and ramp-detection skill are the right metrics.

**Region-specific extremes.** Saharan dust (Sirocco/Chili winds) is the standout Tunisian angle: the March 2022 storm halved the Spanish national PV fleet's capacity factor for over two weeks (80% peak-day drop; Micheli et al. 2024), and West-Africa dust reduced GHI ~18% while modeling dust cut GHI estimation error ~75% (ACP 2025). CAMS provides free 5-day dust-AOD forecasts (0.4°, hourly, via Atmosphere Data Store) and Solcast models soiling from PM2.5/PM10. DustNet (GMD, 2025) beats CAMS at 24-h Saharan AOD and trains in under 8 minutes on a desktop. Heat derating (PV output falls with cell temperature) is easily added via pvlib temperature models.

### 4. Data availability (feasibility core)
- **Open-Meteo**: free (non-commercial) forecast, historical (ERA5, 80+ yr), ensemble, and satellite-radiation APIs; EUMETSAT MSG/SARAH3 covers Africa; provides GHI/DNI/DHI, global tilted irradiance, and CAMS dust/air-quality. No key required for reasonable volumes.
- **NASA POWER**: global, free, hourly solar since 2001, 0.5°; REST API. Best for long training histories anywhere in Tunisia.
- **PVGIS**: free, Europe+Africa, TMY + hourly time series + PV yield; ideal for synthetic/cold-start histories.
- **CAMS** radiation (McClear clear-sky + all-sky) and aerosol/dust forecasts: free with registration; Meteosat field-of-view includes Tunisia.
- **Desert proxy PV**: DKASC Alice Springs (free, 15+ yr, 5-min, 40+ arrays, arid climate ~ Tunisia) is the single best transferable training set; also Adrar (Algeria), NREL PVDAQ, Ausgrid, UK-PV, PVOutput.org.
- **Tunisia**: 895 MW national PV (2025, IRENA); residential rooftop PV ~500 MW + 130 MW on MV/HV networks (STEG, end-June 2026); **no open STEG/ANME generation or per-region installation data**; AERONET at Tunis-Carthage, Ben Salem, Medenine; net-metering surplus capped at 30% of annual production (Decree 2016-1123); several 2023–2026 Tunisian-site forecasting papers exist (Rjim Matouk; southern Tunisia Informer-LSTM + Monte Carlo; Sfax FSEG rooftop; El Akarit/Gabès; Tunisian smart-home LSTM-HHO).
- **Libraries**: pvlib-python, quartz-solar-forecast (OCF, pip, GBT on 9 Open-Meteo NWP vars, trained on 25,000 sites, ~13% MAE ex-night), PVNet, NeuralForecast/StatsForecast, GluonTS, Darts, MAPIE, sktime, NGBoost.

### 5. Ranked project concepts (creativity × feasibility)

**#1 — "DustCast": dust-and-soiling-aware probabilistic rooftop PV forecaster with cleaning decision support.**
Core: pvlib clear-sky baseline + gradient-boosting residual model using Open-Meteo weather **plus CAMS dust-AOD forecasts** as features; conformal (MAPIE/ACI) prediction intervals; a decision layer that flags dust-driven production drops and recommends panel-cleaning timing. Differentiation: almost no student team will ingest Saharan-dust forecasts or output a cleaning/curtailment decision — this is authentically North-African and hits uncertainty + decision support. Data/libs: Open-Meteo, CAMS ADS, pvlib, XGBoost, MAPIE; train/validate on DKASC desert data + NASA POWER for a Tunisian city. Scope: very achievable. Risk: soiling ground-truth is scarce — frame soiling as a modeled/relative index, not a validated absolute. Demo: live map of a Tunisian region, forecast with uncertainty band widening on an incoming dust plume, and a "clean panels Thursday" recommendation.

**#2 — "National upscaler": estimate & forecast aggregate Tunisian rooftop PV from a handful of sites.**
Core: simulate N virtual rooftop sites across Tunisian governorates with pvlib (real tilt/azimuth/capacity + Open-Meteo/CAMS), monitor only a small representative sample, and upscale (yield × capacity, cluster-based à la Pierro/AEMO/OCF) to a regional/national total with conformal intervals; show reserve-sizing implications. Differentiation: directly solves the brief's national multi-scale + grid-impact requirement and mirrors what STEG actually lacks. Data/libs: pvlib, Open-Meteo, NeuralForecast, MAPIE; DKASC/PVOutput for realism. Scope: achievable; the simulation removes the missing-data blocker. Risk: without real Tunisian per-site data it's a simulation — be transparent and validate the upscaling method on a real multi-site set (Ausgrid/UK-PV). Demo: live national map with per-governorate and national forecast + uncertainty + "MW of reserve needed."

**#3 — "Cold-start forecaster" using foundation models + physics-synthetic history.**
Core: reproduce the 2026 physics-informed-synthetic-history idea — generate a pvlib/PVGIS synthetic history from a new rooftop's metadata, feed as context to a zero-shot foundation model (Chronos-2/TimesFM/TabPFN-TS) to forecast plants with zero operating history. Differentiation: genuinely novel, matches Tunisia's constant stream of new unmonitored rooftops. Data/libs: PVGIS, pvlib, Chronos/TimesFM (HuggingFace), DKASC for validation. Scope: moderate. Risk: foundation-model inference latency/compute — mitigate with the smaller checkpoints (TiRex 35M, TTM). Demo: enter a new address + panel specs → instant 48-h forecast with no history.

**#4 — "Nowcaster": Meteosat/sky-image very-short-term forecast for a Tunisian site.**
Core: cloud-index nowcasting from EUMETSAT MSG (via Open-Meteo satellite-radiation or CAMS) with optical-flow or a small U-Net (HelioNet-style), 15–120 min ahead; optionally fuse a low-cost sky camera. Differentiation: multi-temporal-scale coverage (the sub-hour end) and an IoT hook. Data/libs: Open-Meteo satellite API, SKIPP'D for pretraining, OpenCV optical flow. Scope: optical-flow version is feasible; full DL nowcasting is stretch. Risk: real-time satellite latency (~15 min) and image plumbing. Demo: animated cloud field over Tunisia with a 30-min-ahead irradiance/PV trace.

**#5 — "Decision-focused" forecaster trained to minimize reserve/imbalance cost, not RMSE.**
Core: implement smart predict-then-optimize — train the PV forecaster with a loss tied to a simple downstream decision (reserve procurement or PV-battery/30%-surplus dispatch under Tunisian net-metering), showing lower operating cost than an RMSE-trained model. Differentiation: methodologically sophisticated, rarely seen in hackathons, directly "decision support." Data/libs: NeuralForecast/PyTorch + cvxpy; DKASC/NASA POWER. Scope: moderate-hard. Risk: DFL is fiddly — keep the optimization convex/small; a piecewise-linear asymmetric cost is a safe fallback. Demo: side-by-side "RMSE model vs decision-focused model" with imbalance cost.

**#6 — "GridSight": spatio-temporal GNN for a network of Tunisian sites.**
Core: build a graph over simulated/real multi-site PV and train a GCLSTM/DEST-GNN-style model for regional multi-hour forecasting exploiting cloud-motion correlations, with conformal intervals. Differentiation: strongest on the "spatial correlation" science. Data/libs: PyTorch-Geometric, DKASC/Ausgrid, MAPIE. Scope: hardest build — recommend only if the team is strong in DL. Risk: graph construction + training time inside a hackathon. Demo: map showing a cloud front propagating and forecasts updating across neighboring sites.

**#7 — "BTM disaggregator": recover hidden rooftop PV from a feeder net-load signal.**
Core: unsupervised/semi-supervised separation of solar from a simulated net-load feeder using a clear-sky "solar exemplar," estimating invisible BTM PV. Differentiation: tackles observability, a real operator pain-point. Scope: moderate. Risk: needs a plausible net-load signal — simulate load + pvlib PV. Demo: net-load curve split into load + hidden solar in real time.

**#8 — IoT monitoring node feeding the platform (optional differentiator for any of the above).**
Core: an ESP32 + current/irradiance/temperature sensor low-cost node that streams a real rooftop's production to the platform, closing the loop (live data improves the forecast — Open Climate Fix's GSoC 2024 Enphase-integration project targeted ~20% more accurate forecasts from live inverter data). Differentiation: leverages the student's embedded/IoT strength and makes the demo tangible. Scope: a single node is very achievable. Risk: none fatal; keep it one node. Demo: physical node → live dashboard point overlaid on the forecast band.

## Recommendations
1. **Build Concept #1 (DustCast) as the core, and fold in Concept #2's upscaling as the "national" view and Concept #8's IoT node as the live-data hook.** This single integrated platform hits every brief requirement — multi-scale spatial (site → governorate → national), multi-temporal (nowcast → day-ahead), uncertainty (conformal), grid impact (reserve/ramp), decision support (cleaning + curtailment) — with a genuinely Tunisian dust angle and a hardware differentiator.
2. **Baseline first, then differentiate.** Day 1: pvlib clear-sky + XGBoost residual on NASA POWER/Open-Meteo for one Tunisian city, wrapped in MAPIE conformal intervals — this alone is a complete, defensible forecaster. Only then add CAMS dust features, the upscaling map, and the IoT node.
3. **Use free data end-to-end and validate transfer on desert data.** Train/validate on DKASC Alice Springs (arid analogue) and NASA POWER for a Tunisian site; be explicit that no open Tunisian generation data exists and that the national view is a transparent simulation calibrated on real multi-site data.
4. **Report operator metrics, not RMSE.** Show CRPS/pinball, interval coverage (PICP) and sharpness, plus a ramp-detection and reserve-shortfall number — this is what makes judges see "grid-ready."
5. **Thresholds that change the plan:** if real Tunisian per-site data appears, switch the national view from simulation to real upscaling; if compute is tight, drop the GNN/foundation-model options (#3, #6) and keep boosting+conformal; if the sky-camera/IoT integration slips, keep the software node simulated.

## Caveats
- **No open Tunisian PV generation or installation dataset exists** (confirmed: no STEG/ANME open-data equivalent to Australia's CER postcode data). Any "national" forecast will be a simulation or an upscaling method validated on foreign data — state this openly.
- **Foundation models and transformers do not reliably beat gradient boosting for site-level solar** in current literature; treat them as a creative cold-start/spatial add-on, not the accuracy engine.
- **Soiling ground-truth is scarce**; dust-driven irradiance loss is well-supported, but panel-soiling loss should be presented as a modeled index, not a validated measurement.
- Open-Meteo's free tier is for non-commercial use with rate limits; CAMS and NASA POWER have latency (CAMS ~2 days for reanalysis; POWER solar 5–7 days) — fine for training, plan around it for "live" demos by using forecast (not reanalysis) endpoints.
- Some cited results (dust halving Spain's fleet capacity factor; West-Africa GHI reductions; CAISO reserve costs) are from other countries; they establish the mechanism and order of magnitude, not Tunisian-specific values. The Tunisia PV capacity figures come with a known ambiguity: the Ministry has separately cited "~400 MW installed vs 70 MW operational" for low-voltage PV (Dec 2025), which appears to conflate registered pipeline with commissioned capacity — the ~500 MW residential figure is the more recent STEG statement (end-June 2026).
